House washing

A low-pressure wash with a house-wash detergent that lifts green algae, mildew, pollen and road film from siding without blasting it.

Long Island's humid summers and salt air leave a green and black film on the north and shaded sides of most homes. Washing it with high pressure can force water behind siding, chip paint and leave stripes, so we don't do that.

We apply a house-wash detergent at low pressure, give it time to work, then rinse the whole house from top to bottom. The detergent does the cleaning, not the force of the water.

What's included

  • Pre-soaking shrubs and plantings, and rinsing them again when we finish
  • Detergent applied at low pressure to siding, trim, soffits and gutter faces we can reach from the ground
  • Hand brushing on stubborn spots around doors, vents and lights within reach
  • A full top-to-bottom rinse, including a rinse of exterior window glass
  • A walk-around with you, or photos if you're not home

Surfaces we clean

  • Vinyl siding
  • Aluminum siding
  • Painted wood clapboard and shingles (low pressure only)
  • Brick and stone veneer
  • Stucco and EIFS (low pressure only)

What to expect

Algae, mildew, cobwebs, pollen and everyday dirt come off, and the siding's original color comes back. Washing once a year keeps buildup from returning in force.

Limits

  • We don't clean roofs or walk on them.
  • We work from the ground. Areas we can't reach safely are left out and noted on your quote.
  • Chalky oxidation on old aluminum siding, rust stains, irrigation stains and paint overspray may not come off with washing.
  • Loose or peeling paint can flake off during a wash. We point it out before we start.
